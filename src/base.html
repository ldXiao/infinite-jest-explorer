How to use the references. Each citation shows an estimated page and a short phrase quoted exactly from the text, for example ≈p. 157 · Jim not that way Jim.

Phrases are exact text from the edition in your project (Back Bay Books, 2006). Search the phrase in your e-reader to jump straight to the passage. That works in any edition.

Page numbers are estimates for the Back Bay paperback, where the main text runs pp. 3–981 and the endnotes run to p. 1079. They come from where the passage sits in your ePub, checked against known section starts: the opening (p. 3), “Wardine” (p. 37), Marathe on the outcropping (p. 87), B.S. 1960 Tucson (p. 157), the list of Subsidized years (p. 223), Eschaton (p. 321) and the last page (p. 981). Expect them to be within a few pages.

Caveats. Some links are hints rather than statements (for example Avril and C.T.). Some accounts come from unreliable narrators, such as Molly Notkin’s version of Joelle’s past. The descriptions say so where it matters. This page contains spoilers.
